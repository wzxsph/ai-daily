## [DeepMind Paper Argues LLMs Cannot Make Conceptual Leaps for Science](https://openreview.net/challenge?redirect=%2Fforum%3Fid%3DklU4737opt) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A position paper from DeepMind researchers, including Tom Zahavy, argues that large language models are fundamentally incapable of making the intuitive &\#x27;jumps&\#x27; between distant concepts that drive major scientific breakthroughs. The paper contends that the lossy nature of language-based knowledge encoding prevents LLMs from formulating the new foundational axioms required for paradigm-shifting discoveries. This directly challenges the prevailing narrative that scaling LLMs will eventually lead to AI-driven scientific discovery, potentially redirecting research investment toward alternative architectures or hybrid systems. It frames a core limitation not as a temporary engineering problem but as a structural one inherent to text-based training. The argument hinges on &\#x27;jumps&\#x27; — the ability to formulate new axioms rather than merely execute mathematics given existing ones. Research suggests LLMs internally encode ~40% more factual knowledge than they express, indicating the limitation lies in generation and conceptual bridging, not purely in knowledge storage.

hackernews · theanonymousone · Aug 5, 11:01 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49181083)

**Background**: A &\#x27;position paper&\#x27; in AI research is a publication where authors stake out and defend a specific viewpoint on an open question, rather than presenting new empirical results. Scientific &\#x27;conceptual leaps&\#x27; or &\#x27;jumps&\#x27; refer to moments when a thinker reframes an entire field by introducing new axioms — Einstein&\#x27;s Special Relativity, built on postulates about light speed, is a classic example. LLMs are neural networks trained on vast text corpora to predict and generate language, and their potential to automate or accelerate scientific reasoning has been a major topic of debate since models like GPT-4 began assisting with research tasks.

## [OncoTriad-QA: Multimodal Benchmark for Pan-Cancer Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02615) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers introduced OncoTriad-QA, a patient-level benchmark containing 86.1k questions across 9,281 TCGA patient cases spanning 32 cancer cohorts, integrating CT/MRI radiology, whole-slide histopathology, somatic mutations, copy-number alterations, DNA methylation, bulk RNA-seq, and clinical metadata. They also proposed OncoVLM, a reference multimodal model that uses learned projectors to map modality-native evidence \(radiology, pathology, methylation, RNA-seq\) into an LLM interface, which outperforms MedGemma-4B by an average of 10.7 points after fine-tuning. Most existing medical LLM/VLM benchmarks evaluate isolated modalities or narrow image-text tasks, leaving real-world oncology workflows—where clinicians must synthesize imaging, morphological, and molecular evidence—largely untested. OncoTriad-QA addresses this critical gap by providing the first large-scale, patient-level benchmark that demands true cross-modality reasoning across 32 cancer types. The benchmark uses a source-grounded LLM-assisted annotation pipeline with curated labels, diagnostic reports, and molecular profiles as primary sources of truth, combined with automated consistency checks and clinician review. Despite fine-tuning gains, existing general-purpose and medical LLMs remain limited on comprehensive pan-cancer QA, especially when questions require integrating imaging findings, tumor morphology, and molecular evidence simultaneously.

rss · arXiv cs.CL · Aug 5, 04:00

**Background**: The Cancer Genome Atlas \(TCGA\) is a landmark cancer genomics program that molecularly characterized over 11,000 primary cancer samples, providing the multi-omics foundation for this benchmark. Cancer diagnosis in clinical practice requires synthesizing complementary data: whole-slide histopathology \(WSI\) captures tumor morphology at the cellular level, while CT/MRI radiology reveals structural and spatial information, and molecular assays such as somatic mutation calling, copy-number analysis, DNA methylation profiling, and RNA sequencing reveal the underlying genomic and transcriptional drivers. Integrating these modalities is essential for accurate diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment selection, which is precisely the multi-evidence reasoning that current medical LLM benchmarks fail to evaluate.
