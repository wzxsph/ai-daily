## [Munich Court Rules Suno Violated Copyrights, Rejects Fair Use](https://the-decoder.com/german-court-rules-ai-music-generator-suno-violated-copyrights-rejects-fair-use-defense/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A Munich court ruled that AI music generator Suno infringed copyrights through both its training process and its generated outputs. The court found six songs reproducibly stored in Suno&\#x27;s models and rejected both Germany&\#x27;s text-and-data-mining \(TDM\) exception and the U.S. fair use defense. This ruling directly challenges the legal foundations under which generative AI companies train their models on copyrighted content, signaling that neither European TDM exceptions nor U.S. fair use arguments provide automatic safe harbor for AI music generators. It has major implications for Suno, OpenAI, and the broader generative AI industry as similar lawsuits proceed worldwide. The court identified six specific songs that were reproducibly memorized in Suno&\#x27;s models, providing concrete evidence of copyright infringement at the output stage. The ruling is not yet final, and several substantive legal questions remain open, suggesting Suno may appeal or that further proceedings could refine the holding.

**Background**: Suno is an AI music generation platform that converts text prompts into complete songs with vocals and instrumentation. Text-and-data-mining \(TDM\) is an EU-derived copyright exception that permits automated analysis of copyright-protected works without the rights holder&\#x27;s consent, and it has been a key legal defense used by AI companies training in Europe. Fair use, by contrast, is a U.S. doctrine that allows limited use of copyrighted material for purposes such as commentary, criticism, or transformative use, and has been central to U.S. lawsuits like Kadrey v. Meta involving AI training on copyrighted books. This Munich ruling represents one of the first European court decisions to comprehensively reject both defenses in a single case against a generative AI music service.
